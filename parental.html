Going further

The strongest protection, and what it takes to turn on

This is a deliberate, one-time extra step — not something that's on by default for every linked child.

Android

1

This tier is set up once, at the start — when a phone is first being configured for your child, not something you flip on a phone already in daily use.

2

It involves a factory reset and scanning a one-time QR code during the device's initial setup screens, which provisions Unashamed as the phone's device owner at the operating-system level. Get the QR code →

3

From then on, Force Stop and Uninstall are greyed out by Android itself for this app — confirmed on real hardware, not just a setting inside the app — and install-lock can be turned on as a separate switch.

iPhone

1

The child's iPhone needs to already be set up as a child account under your Apple ID through Family Sharing — done in Apple's own Settings app, a prerequisite Unashamed doesn't set up itself.

2

When setting up Unashamed on the child's phone, choose that it's for your child when asked, then confirm with your Screen Time passcode.

3

That turns on both the deletion lock and the install lock at once; either can later be turned off independently, but only from your side, not the child's.

What's different between iOS and Android

Android supports the full set of remote controls above. On iOS, parent–child linking and the restrictions Apple's platform exposes to apps are supported; anything Apple keeps private to the device itself is outside what any app — ours included — can remotely see or enforce.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this spyware — can I read their messages?

No. There's no message, photo, or browsing-history access. You get a specific, visible set of controls: app blocks, time limits, bedtime blocks, a study timetable, and app-usage insight — nothing beyond that.

Does my child know I'm linked to their account?

Yes, always. Linking happens through an invite your child accepts themselves, and every restriction you set shows up clearly as "set by parent" in their own app.

Can my child remove a restriction I set?

No. A parent-set block, limit, or schedule can only be removed by the parent who set it — your child can manage their own self-set rules, but not yours.

Can my child unlink from me or link to someone else instead?

No. Only the parent can end an active link, and a child already linked to a parent can't accept a second invite — closing the obvious way around oversight.

Does this work the same on iOS and Android?

Android supports the full set of controls. On iOS, linking and monitoring work within what Apple's platform exposes to any app — some device-level restrictions aren't available there to us or anyone else.

Does it cost extra per child?

No — one family subscription covers every child you link, no per-child billing.

What if my child uninstalls the app or clears its data?

Almost everything — block state, your restrictions, the family link itself — lives on our servers, not on the phone. Clearing app data or reinstalling and signing back in re-applies everything exactly as you set it. If you've turned on the strongest protection tier (above), uninstalling isn't possible at all — the app is greyed out in the OS's own app settings. Without it, uninstalling and never signing back in is a real gap, same as with any app that isn't locked down that way.

Can I stop new apps from being installed without my approval?

Yes — turning on install lock (part of the strongest protection tier) blocks new app installs on the child's phone until you turn it back off. It's a separate switch from the uninstall/force-stop lock, so you can use either on its own.

What about a factory reset?

A factory reset removes any app, including ours, and that's true even with the strongest protection tier turned on — deliberately. No app on the market can prevent a factory reset, and we didn't try to; it also means you always keep the ability to factory-reset a phone you own.
